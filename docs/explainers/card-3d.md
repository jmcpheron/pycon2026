# The card in 3D — assembly, exploded view, and parts

> *[your voice here] one-line hook about the Onshape STEP being the canonical source of truth — and how `stepforge` turns it into every visual on this page.*

The model below is the literal AP242 STEP file exported from Onshape ([`jmcpheron-card.step`](https://github.com/jmcpheron/pycon2026/blob/main/jmcpheron-card.step)), routed through Python in three different ways:

1. **`cascadio`** converts STEP → GLB in one call, preserving Onshape's per-part colors (the deep-blue gears and gray card halves you see below).
2. **`trimesh`** decomposes the assembly into seven named parts and writes one GLB + STL + PNG per part.
3. **OpenSCAD**, driven from Python, renders the per-frame exploded-view animation that lives further down the page.

All three are wired together by `uv run stepforge explode --in jmcpheron-card.step --out docs/assets/card/`.

## Exploded view

The card-layered explode strategy lifts the two card halves to ±25 mm on Z and fans the five gears between them, sorted by their X position. The bottom card's shaft-hole pattern becomes visible — five holes spaced 15 mm apart, which is the gear-train pitch.

## Parts

There are seven instances of three distinct parts. Both card halves share the name "Part 1" in Onshape; the five gears all share "Spur gear (40 teeth)" — but two of them are actually a **tall-hub variant** for the compound stages (8 mm Z extent vs the standard 3 mm). ## What's lost in the STEP export

Onshape's STEP exporter is faithful about geometry and colors. It's quiet about everything else:

- **Tooth counts and gear ratios** don't survive as structured properties — only as substrings in part names like `Spur gear (40 teeth)`. The ratio chain documented in [gear-ratios](gear-ratios.md) comes from the canonical [`card.py`](../../src/explainers/card.py), not from the STEP.
- **Mate connectors** survive as nameless `AXIS2_PLACEMENT_3D` placement frames. You can recover the *positions* (we do — each gear's centroid in the manifest), but not the *kind* of mate (concentric, planar, distance-along-axis).
- **Feature history** is irrelevant for a B-rep export — STEP is the result, not the recipe.

If you want any of these back in the loop, commit a sidecar `jmcpheron-card.meta.toml` next to the STEP. `stepforge inspect` merges it into the report; `stepforge explode` joins it to each rendered part by name.
